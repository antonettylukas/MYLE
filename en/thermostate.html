Smart radiator thermostats

✓ Last checked: November 2026

They go on your radiators in place of the normal dials and heat according to your plan. You can control them via the app or directly on the device.

The advantages are obvious: shortly before you get home, your flat is already warm. While you're at work, the thermostats turn the heating down by themselves. So you simply can't forget to turn it down any more, and you save money. All you do is set up a weekly heating plan once. And the thermostats themselves are usually cheap.

How to set up your heating plan

To start with, a simple plan for weekdays and one for the weekend is enough:

  1. Warm shortly before you get up, for example 21 degrees
  2. When you leave the house, down to 16 to 17 degrees. Better not right off, or the walls cool down
  3. Warm again shortly before you get home
  4. 16 to 18 degrees in the bedroom at night

Pricier thermostats

Better with a hub

I haven't found a pricier thermostat without a hub that does well in tests and is available on amazon.co.uk. The thermostats that do best in tests need a hub: have a look at the “With a hub” group.

These thermostats talk to a small hub, also called a gateway, that is connected to your router. It is a one-off extra cost. For some models a device you may already have is enough, for example a HomePod mini or a Google Nest Hub (2nd generation). In return these thermostats do best in tests, and according to the manufacturers the batteries last up to two years.

Questions and answers

How much do I really save?

With a heating plan, around 10 per cent of your heating costs, according to co2online. As a rule of thumb, every degree less saves around 6 per cent of heating energy. How much it is for you depends on how often you're out and how you heat at the moment. For a poorly insulated 70 m² flat, Stiftung Warentest (issue 9/2023) reckons on around €100 a year. Work it out for yourself in the calculator below.

With or without a hub: which suits me?

Without a hub is the easiest start: unpack, connect to Wi-Fi, done. That suits you if you only want to control a few radiators and it should be cheap. With a hub you pay a bit more once, but you get the thermostats that do best in tests. You can also add window contacts or a wall thermostat later. That's worth it above all if you want to fit out the whole flat.

Why are there so few Wi-Fi thermostats?

Wi-Fi uses more power than the radio types that thermostats with a hub use. Important: your router needs 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi. That's the case with almost all routers.

Will they fit my radiators?

Almost all normal radiator valves. Most have an M30 x 1.5 standard thread, and all the models here fit on that. For other valves, for example from Danfoss, adapters are often included, or you can get them for little money. For underfloor heating, on the other hand, you need a room thermostat on the wall.

Can I fit them as a tenant?

Usually yes, because you don't change anything permanently. You just unscrew the old dial and screw the new thermostat on. You don't have to drain any water, and you don't need a tradesperson either. Keep the old dials safe and screw them back on when you move out. If in doubt, have a quick word with your landlord.

What if I come home earlier?

No problem: you briefly turn the thermostat up or tap warmer in the app. From the next point in your heating plan, everything runs by itself again. Some systems even use your phone's location to tell when you're on your way home.

Doesn't the flat get too cold during the day?

Not if you turn it down to 16 to 17 degrees instead of right off. It shouldn't drop below 16 degrees: otherwise the walls cool down, moisture settles on them, and the risk of mould goes up. Heating the flat back up afterwards costs less than heating it all day long.

What about open windows?

Most of the picks here notice when the temperature suddenly drops while you air the room, and turn down for a while. The Bosch thermostat needs a window contact for this. So you're not heating the outdoors.
