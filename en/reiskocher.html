Rice cooker

A small appliance that cooks rice by itself and keeps it warm afterwards. With many models you can steam vegetables or fish in a basket on top at the same time.

Rice and water in, press the button, done. You don't have to stand next to it: with the right amount of water nothing boils over, and at Stiftung Warentest nothing burned on in any of the cookers. Once the water has been absorbed, it switches to keep-warm by itself. So the rice waits for you, not you for the rice.

Questions and answers

How much water do I add?

Lines inside the pot show you. You measure the rice with the cup that comes with it and pour in water up to the matching line. Stick to that and it's right almost every time.

Do I have to wash the rice first?

Yes, a quick rinse is worth it: then it sticks less and is less likely to boil over. Tip it into a sieve and rinse it with cold water until the water is almost clear.

Can I cook vegetables or fish in it too?

Yes, if your appliance has a steamer basket. Almost all do, the Tsuki Mini doesn't. It goes on top of the pot, and on many cookers vegetables or fish steam in it while the rice cooks below. Whether the vegetables stay crisp depends on the cooker: at Stiftung Warentest, only six cookers managed crisp green broccoli (issue 5/2026).

How long can the rice stay warm?

Not too long. Germany's Federal Institute for Risk Assessment advises eating food that is kept warm no later than three hours after cooking, and it should stay above 65 degrees. Put leftovers in the fridge quickly and eat them within a day. Cooked rice shouldn't sit around lukewarm, or germs can grow.

Is the coating safe?

On most cookers, yes. But Stiftung Warentest found that much more aluminium leaches out of the coating on two rice cookers from Reishunger than on the other cookers, even though the limit is met (issue 5/2026). So I don't recommend those. Whatever the cooker: no metal spoons and no scouring pads, so that the coating lasts a long time.

Does rice from a rice cooker taste better?

It depends on the cooker. A rice cooker controls the heat itself and switches to keep-warm as soon as the water has been absorbed. But not every cooker does this well: at Stiftung Warentest only 6 of 13 rice cookers cooked rice well, including the two test winners (issue 5/2026). Nothing burned on in any of them.
