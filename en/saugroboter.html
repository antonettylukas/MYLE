Robot vacuum and mop

✓ Last checked: November 2026

A robot that vacuums your home on its own and mops too, if you like. Afterwards it drives back to its base station, which empties it and, on mopping robots, also washes the mop roller or the mop pads.

The best part is something very few people know: you set a schedule in the app, and the robot cleans while you're at work. Every day you come home to a clean flat and hardly have to do anything for it. After 14 years, that's still a massive upgrade in quality of life for me.

How to set it up

Take a quarter of an hour once, and after that everything runs by itself:

  1. Install the app and let the robot drive through your home once. It draws a map as it goes.
  2. Create a schedule, for example vacuum and mop Monday to Friday at 9 am.
  3. Mark rugs and carpets in the app so it only vacuums there and doesn't mop.

Questions and answers

Which tests do you base your robot picks on?

For robot vacuums and mops I rely on the detailed tests at smarthomeassistent.de. There every robot goes through the same trials, from carpet and hair to obstacles and door thresholds. Their grades work like German school grades: 1.0 is the best. I recommend the models that do best in their price range there, plus my own experience from 14 years. If a robot is tested again after an update, for example because its navigation has improved, the newer test is the one that counts for me.

Do I need the mopping function?

With lots of hard floors like tiles, laminate or parquet, definitely: the floor stays clean and you never fill a bucket again. If you mostly have carpet, or you like mopping yourself anyway, a robot that only vacuums is enough. That one often costs only half as much.

Mop roller or rotating mops: which is better?

Both can mop very well: at smarthomeassistent.de, for example, the MOVA Z60 with a roller and the Dreame L40s Pro Ultra with mop pads each get the top grade of 1.0 for mopping. The roller picks the dirty water straight back up, so it has to go back to the base station to be washed less often. The mop pads get into edges and corners better, but tend to spread dirty water around. If you have lots of corners, nooks and furniture edges, go for mop pads. If you mainly have open floor space, a roller is a good fit. Right now, though, I haven't found a roller model on amazon.co.uk that mops well in tests, so for now there isn't one on this page.

Does it get stuck on cables or socks?

Models with a camera usually recognise cables, socks and shoes and drive around them, and many recognise pets too. Cheap models without a camera are more likely to run over a cable or get stuck, so you quickly tidy up first. A tangle of cables under the desk is best sorted out once, whatever the model.

Can I start it when I'm not at home?

Yes, and that's exactly the trick. In the app you set up a schedule, for example Monday to Friday at 9 am. That's how I've been doing it for years. You can also start it from your phone when you're out.

Can it get over door thresholds and carpet edges?

Most models manage thresholds up to about 2 cm, good models like the Dreame X60 about 4 to 5 cm in tests. On carpet a mopping robot lifts its mop pads or roller so nothing gets wet.

What do I still have to do myself?

Change the dust bag every few weeks and clean the brush now and then. With a mopping robot you also top up the fresh water and empty the dirty water as soon as it tells you. But don't leave the dirty water standing for days, or it starts to smell.

What does it cost to run?

Bags, filters, brushes and the mop pads or roller wear out. According to a cost check by netzwelt.de, a robot vacuum and mop with a base station comes to about €45 to €185 a year, without mopping about €25 to €120. Electricity adds about €15 to €25 a year, a bit more for base stations with hot water and drying.

Does it work on several floors?

It remembers several maps. The base station stays in one place, though. You carry it to the other floor, and it can only empty itself again at the base station.

How loud is it?

Usually about as loud as a conversation, so quieter than a normal vacuum cleaner. And because it runs while you're out, you won't notice it anyway. Only the base station is really loud for a few seconds while it empties the robot.
